# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

Welcome to the OctoAcme project management documentation hub. This README serves as the entry point for understanding how OctoAcme plans, executes, and continuously improves its projects.

## Overview

OctoAcme follows an end-to-end project lifecycle that guides teams from the first spark of an idea through to production release and retrospective learning. The lifecycle consists of six stages: **Initiation**, **Planning**, **Execution & Tracking**, **Risk Management & Communication**, **Release & Deployment**, and **Retrospective & Continuous Improvement**. Each stage has defined artifacts, owners, and handoff criteria to ensure nothing slips through the cracks.

**Roles and ownership** are explicit at every stage. The core personas are the Project Manager (PM), who coordinates delivery schedules, risks, and communications; the Technical Lead, who owns the architectural direction and technical decisions; Contributors (developers and QA), who implement and validate work; and Stakeholders, who provide input and sign off on key decisions. Handoffs between roles are documented, and a designated decision log ensures that key choices and their rationale are always traceable.

**Communication** is intentional and structured. The team holds twice-weekly standups, weekly PM–Product Manager syncs, and monthly stakeholder updates. Risks and blockers are escalated promptly through a defined escalation path rather than waiting for the next scheduled meeting. All significant decisions are captured in the decision log so that anyone can understand the "why" behind the direction the project has taken.

**Quality assurance** is built into every stage rather than bolted on at the end. Acceptance criteria and a clear Definition of Done are established during planning, code and design reviews happen throughout execution, and a formal release-readiness checklist gates every deployment. Retrospectives close the loop by feeding process improvements back into these very documents, keeping the team's knowledge base current and actionable.
